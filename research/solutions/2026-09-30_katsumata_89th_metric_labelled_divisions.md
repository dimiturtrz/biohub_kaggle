# 89th place — label the candidates with the official metric, then compute the break-even

Source: topic **#744490** (Takahiro Katsumata, 89th of 4,020, silver; private 0.928).

**This is the one team that made the division term pay on our own chassis** — the same
`thtennant/biohub-frontier947-readmit-v1` family we forked — with **no new detector and no new linker**, by
learning *which* division candidates to keep. It is also the cleanest published instance of a technique our
own never-tried list named: **labels generated by replaying the official scorer**.

| submission | public | private |
|---|---|---|
| public chassis, byte-identical output | 0.946 | 0.913 |
| + their coordinate head | 0.953 | 0.920 |
| **+ DIV-C2 division reselection** (τ 0.18 / 0.30) | 0.963 / 0.966 | **0.928 / 0.927** |
| + 1% pruning, + z-window 3 (final picks) | 0.967 / 0.966 | 0.928 / 0.927 |
| *third detector seed + head + reselection (not picked)* | 0.959 | **0.935** |

Of their public climb 0.812 → 0.967, **+0.132 came from reproducing public work and +0.023 from their own
changes**: third seed +0.002, coordinate head +0.007, division reselection +0.013, pruning +0.001.

## 1. Why divisions pay far more than their 10% weight

> "The division term has weight 0.1, but the hidden set rewards it: removing every division from the 0.926
> public notebook cost **−0.022 on Public against −0.0067 locally**."

A 3.3× discrepancy between the local harness and the board, in the same direction as our own
`division proxy BLIND` finding (our test proxy contained 7 divisions; theirs under-reads by 3×). 213th
measured the same thing from the other end: disabling post-hoc divisions entirely is **−0.035**.

**The break-even arithmetic**, which is the part to keep:

> Adding a candidate that is right with probability `p` changes micro division J by
> `(1/D) · [p − (1−p)·J]`, with `D = TP + FP + FN`. So the break-even is **`p* = J / (1 + J)`**.

Train `J = 0.214` → **`τ ≈ 0.18`**. Our own replayed currency (one division = 5.5e-4 = 74 edges; break-even
precision for an added division 25–32%) agrees with this to within the difference in `J`: `0.214/1.214 =
0.176`, our 25–32% came from a different operating `J`. **Same formula, arrived at independently.**

And the asymmetry that follows: "with a pooled division J near 0.2, **losing one TP costs about 5× what
removing one FP gains**."

## 2. DIV-C2 — labels from the official metric, not from geometry

**Candidates**: the chassis's own gates with the symmetry condition dropped and divergence loosened
2.25 µm → **1.0 µm**. A candidate is (parent at t, its current child at t+1, an orphan at t+1). The original
rule's picks are always included, so the model can only add.

**Labels — this is the technique.**

> "For each candidate we **replay the rest of the pipeline with and without it and score both with the
> official division scorer**. A TP gain is positive, an FP gain without a TP gain is negative, and anything
> else is left unlabelled."

> "This caught divisions predicted **one frame early** (a TP for the metric), which had been **20–30% of
> positives** and were mislabelled."

That last sentence is the whole argument for the method. A geometric or annotation-derived label calls a
one-frame-early division wrong; the metric calls it a true positive. **Between a quarter and a third of the
available positives are invisible to any label that is not the metric itself.** Our own never-tried item
read "labels = every metric-scorable edge"; this is that item, executed, on divisions, for +0.008 private.

**Model (M-A)**: an **L2 logistic regression on 18 standardised features** — geometry (parent, sister and
child distances, symmetry, sister separation growth at t+2 and t+3), track lengths, nearby track ends, ILP
probabilities, the DeepCenter score, local density. Trained on **157 labelled candidates (31 positive)** from
40 videos.

A logistic regression on 157 rows is worth **+0.008 private**. Compare what we spent on detector training.

**Why not richer features**: U-Net point features for divisions scored 0.958–0.964 public against M-A's
0.963–0.966 — "in-sample features: AUC gain +0.068 in-sample, **+0.006 on unseen videos**." And retraining
M-A on 195 videos *lost* 0.0042 locally: "extra videos are **6% positive vs 20%**; probabilities fell." The
candidate distribution, not the count, is what the threshold is calibrated against.

**The ceiling they measured**: an oracle keeping only true candidates adds **+0.0174** locally, a further
+0.0142 over their shipped arm. At τ 0.30, M-A captured **18% of the oracle's gain**. So this axis has ~5×
more in it than they took.

## 3. The τ sweep — the reselection transferred, the tuning did not

| τ | 0.10 | 0.18 | 0.30 | 0.40 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| public | 0.957 | 0.963 | **0.966** | 0.966 |
| private | 0.927 | **0.928** | 0.927 | 0.926 |

The derived break-even (0.18) is the private optimum. Public rewarded pushing past it. **The mechanism
transferred (+0.008 private); the knob fitted to public was worth nothing** — the cleanest instance in any
write-up of the distinction our own post-mortem is built around.

## 4. PRN — monetising the uncapped node-count bonus

> "Adjusted edge J = `J · (1 − 0.1·(N_pred − N_total)/N_total)`, and **the bonus for fewer nodes has no
> cap**. Only about **3% of predicted nodes match an annotated GT node**, so removing nodes that are unlikely
> to be annotated costs little."

A gradient-boosted classifier on **57 test-time features** (position, density, edge probabilities, *which
repair step made each edge*, component statistics) ranks nodes; the lowest **1% per video** are removed —
"typically short weak components at the lateral border." Worth +0.001.

They state the limit and then the honesty:

- Cross-embryo AUC only **0.55–0.69**, and the two train embryos are annotated differently (clustered vs
  scattered lineages). Beyond 1%, one embryo turned negative leave-one-embryo-out, so they stopped.
- "PRN **exploits** the node-count term: it breaks no rule, but a metric fix would remove it."

**Bearing on our prune axis.** We closed prune end to end — `E47` (collateral ~2×, caps +0.021 even
PERFECT), `E48/E49` (GBM FP-recall 0.0846 vs 0.75 needed), `E52` (champion min component already 6),
`E53` (unannotated FPs metric-invisible). Every one of those verdicts holds: 89th's own classifier is at AUC
0.55–0.69, they capped at 1%, and it bought +0.001. **We were right that the axis is small, and we did not
take the small thing.** The frontier's version of the same insight — 18th place's `a`·`q` weighting — is the
larger one.

## 5. The chassis choice, which is their headline lesson

Their **third detector seed** was worth **+0.009 private with or without the head** (public +0.002 / −0.002),
and +0.007 private with reselection on top (public **−0.004**). Their best private, 0.935 (≈ 42nd–48th), was
a submission they did not pick, because once the coordinate head was on, the third-seed chassis "had been
0.002–0.006 lower on Public every time."

> "Across our 20 submissions with Public ≥ 0.955, the Public–Private rank correlation was **0.06**
> (Spearman)."

> "With about 12 Public videos, a Public gap of one or two divisions should not pick the chassis. **Keep one
> final slot for a different chassis.**"

Their split arithmetic: hidden test 40 videos, public ~29% → ~12 public / 28 private. One division TP is
worth ~+0.003 or more on public. A bootstrap gave the reselection effect an SD of **0.0049 on 12 videos and
0.0021 on 28**. "We read 0.001 on Public as noise, yet still let gaps of 0.002–0.006 pick our chassis."

*(Note: they use Kaggle's stated 29%. Our own post-mortem measured the public fraction at ~21% by detection
count. Both can hold if the split is 29% of rows and the private embryo is denser in videos than in rows —
the discrepancy is unresolved and worth flagging wherever either number is used.)*

## 6. Their in-sample statement, and the out-of-sample yardstick

> "The seed-B detector's training metadata lists **all 199 train videos** [...] Up to 25 Sep, **seven of our
> changes gained ≥ +0.005 locally, and none gained ≥ +0.002 on Public**. Division reselection was the
> exception: C2-18 went from +0.0047 locally to +0.010 on Public."

Independent confirmation of `E51` (the proxy is in-sample, offline numbers are ceilings) — and note *which*
change broke the pattern: the one whose labels came from the metric rather than from the local harness.

Their fix was **155 extra train videos never used to fit anything** (the public detectors are in-sample on
them, but their own components were not). That check made them drop one rule and demote another; on private
each would have added +0.001 to +0.002 — "within noise, but in the wrong direction for us."

## 7. Process, worth noting

Pre-registered cards committed **before each submission** fixing the change, the local safety conditions and
how to read the public score, with waivers logged. Visible-run guards: stages before the change
byte-identical to the parent, activation markers present, no fallback, runtime ≤ ~1.10× the parent, valid
CSV. *"Anthropic Claude handled design, audits and some implementation, and OpenAI Codex most of the
implementation. A human authorised every submission and chose the final slots."*

The activation-marker guard is the mechanised form of our own hardest-won harness lesson — assert the flag
LANDED, don't assume.
