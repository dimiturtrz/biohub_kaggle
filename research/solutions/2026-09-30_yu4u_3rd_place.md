## 1. The task as they characterise it

199 training videos cropped from **two** embryos, 100 frames each at 64 × 256 × 256 voxels, spacing
(1.625, 0.40625, 0.40625) µm in (z, y, x) — so all distances are computed in physical coordinates.

Annotation is the whole problem: **~133,000 annotated nodes ≈ 2.8% of the estimated total**, and only
**151 annotated divisions** in the entire training set. Many plainly visible cells carry no label.

The official score is node-count-adjusted **edge Jaccard + 0.1 × division Jaccard**. Their reading of
how sparsity interacts with it:

> Links entirely within unannotated regions are not automatically false positives; incorrect links
> that compete with annotated connections are penalized. A separate adjustment accounts for the
> number of predicted nodes. Detection precision or local link classification accuracy alone
> therefore does not describe the final score.

## 3. Validation

Five-fold CV at **video level**, videos from both embryos distributed across folds, all frames of a
video kept together. OOF predictions generated for detectors, matcher and division models; the
downstream calibration models were also trained with the evaluation fold excluded.

They are explicit about the limits: crops from the same embryo can overlap in space and time across
folds, so the split is **not fully independent**, and *"agreement with the public leaderboard also
does not establish generalization to the private test embryo."* They considered train-on-one-embryo /
validate-on-the-other and chose video-level 5-fold anyway, selecting on CV-and-public-LB improvement
trends.

## 4. Detection (55% of total runtime)

Their stated priority: *"We put particular effort into detection accuracy because every downstream
stage depends on the detected cells."*

**Backbones — deliberately cross-architecture:**

| detector | architecture | heatmap weight |
|---|---|---|
| EfficientNetV2-L | 2.5D U-Net | 0.3 |
| EfficientNet-B7 | 2.5D U-Net | 0.4 |
| SegResNet (MONAI) | 3D residual encoder-decoder | 0.3 |

The 2.5D backbone is reused from their **CZII CryoET Object Identification** solution: a pretrained 2D
encoder over z slices, features pooled along z at each stage, a 3D decoder combining them. Each
encoder input stacks **three neighbouring z slices as channels** so the first layer sees local depth.
Those slices give spatial context only — temporal information enters later, in tracking.

The 3D detector is MONAI SegResNet: 32 initial channels, four encoder stages, one residual block per
stage, **GroupNorm**.

**Training under sparse annotation — the masking scheme.** Target is a Gaussian of **σ = 2 µm** at each
annotated centre. Plain MSE against that target would teach the detector to predict zero at the ~97%
of cells that are real but unlabelled. So:

- extract candidate centres with **Difference of Gaussians at a low threshold**;
- **exclude regions within 6 µm of *unmatched* candidates from the loss** — those cells become neither
  positive nor negative;
- a DoG candidate within **2 µm of an annotated centre** is a duplicate and creates **no** exclusion
  region;
- positive regions around annotated centres **stay supervised even where they overlap an exclusion
  region**;
- MSE averaged **separately over positive and background** regions, background weight **0.5**, so the
  much larger background volume cannot swamp the centre supervision.

DoG is used only to find uncertain background to mask. It adds **no positive pseudo-labels**.

Intensities normalised by the **0.1st / 99.9th percentile of each video**. Crops 48 × 192 × 192.
Augmentation: flips, xy-plane rotations, intensity changes, blur. **Scaling and rotation augmentation
degraded performance.**

**Inference.** Per detector: average the five fold models over **two TTA views** (original, and flipped
along both x and y); combine detectors at the weights above; extract centres by NMS with a
**(3, 7, 7)** kernel and threshold **0.2**.

## 5. Dense flow (A1)

Downsample (z, y, x) = **(1, 4, 4)** → an isotropic grid at 1.625 µm. A small 3D encoder-decoder runs
on it: **local correlations plus soft-argmax** give an initial displacement, which the network refines
with a **residual** prediction. Encoder channels 32 / 64 / 128, **no normalisation layers**.

Training is mostly **self-supervised on real pairs** — image and local-feature consistency after
warping, forward-backward consistency, smoothness — plus **direct displacement supervision on
synthetic pairs** generated with known translations and smooth deformations. Annotated links are used
for endpoint-error evaluation and **checkpoint selection only, never in the loss**.

One flow field, two consumers: correspondence search, and alignment of the images fed to the division
model.

## 6. Matcher (A2)

Own 2.5D U-Net with an **EfficientNetV2-S** encoder, separate from the detectors, same (1, 4, 4) grid
as flow. **64-channel features sampled at each detection from an intermediate decoder stage** — the
full-resolution decoder output is never computed. Self-attention and cross-attention over cell
features and positional information; the head refines scores from appearance cosine similarity, uses
**role embeddings** to distinguish the two frames, and predicts a **null option** (no match).

Training: detections matched to annotated cells are anchors, annotated edges give targets for
**bidirectional cross-entropy**. Unannotated cells remain competing candidates, but absence of
annotation does **not** make them null targets. For a dividing parent, a target is built for **each
daughter with the other daughter excluded from the negative set**.

Candidate export for ordinary links: matcher **top-5** probabilities whose **post-flow distance ≤ 12
µm**; ranks, forward and reverse probabilities, and **margins over competing candidates** pass
downstream.

## 7. Division identification (A3)

Three trained models, not a rule set.

**Parent model** — predicts whether a detection splits in the next frame. Because both division and
ordinary motion change a local region's appearance, neighbouring frames are **aligned to the central
frame** before input; the originals are kept too, *"because a single displacement field cannot fully
represent a one-to-two split."* Five volumes from three timepoints:

```
[ original t−1 , t−1 aligned to t , t , t+1 aligned to t , original t+1 ]
```

At **(1, 2, 2)** downsampling. Converting flow onto that grid accounts for **both displacement units
and the voxel-centre offsets introduced by pooling**. The next frame is sampled with forward flow; the
previous frame's inverse displacement is **approximated by negating the preceding forward flow field**.
A shared EfficientNetV2-S encoder processes each input, corresponding-stage features are concatenated
**in input order**, and a 3D decoder with GroupNorm predicts a parent score at each detection. **The
flow model stays frozen.**

Positives: annotated parents with two children in the next frame. Negatives: ordinary continuing cells
**and daughters immediately after division**. Detections with no GT match **do not enter the negative
loss**. Positive and negative BCE averaged separately. Sampling: **25% frames containing positive
parents, 25% hard-negative frames around divisions, 50% ordinary**. AdamW, lr 1e-4, 20 epochs, EMA
decay 0.995.

**pre / post models** — independent. For a division with parent at t and daughters at t+1, `pre`
identifies the precursor at t−1 and `post` identifies the daughters at t+1. Each takes three original
frames centred on the cell's evaluation time. Their scores are extra evidence for division candidates.

The submission uses the aligned+original parent model for the **primary** candidate pool. A **second**
parent model on three original frames only, together with pre and post, supports ordinary-link
calibration and an **additional division pool at 16 µm radius**. The two pools are calibrated
separately. Division inference averages **five folds × four xy rotations**; OOF evaluation uses the
held-out model per video.

## 8. Calibration — modelling the sparse metric directly

Candidates are either ordinary links or division events (one parent, two daughters). A **parent score
threshold of 0.1** gates division candidate generation; final selection belongs to the optimiser.

Features: distance, bidirectional matching scores, candidate ranks, margins, detection confidence,
parent/pre/post scores, and **aggregated predecessor evidence** over several possible predecessors
(e.g. max of `pre score × matching probability`, and an average weighted by matching probabilities),
plus motion and daughter separation.

**The sparse-annotation trick.** Two probabilities are estimated per candidate:

- **`a`** — probability of **being evaluated** at all, i.e. whether the candidate is subject to scoring
  given the available annotations;
- **`q`** — **conditional** probability of being correct, *given* that it is evaluated.

Then `a · q` is the expected true-positive contribution and `a · (1 − q)` the expected false-positive
contribution. In their words, this *"avoids treating all unevaluated candidates as errors during
training."*

Models: ordinary links by **logistic regression**. Conditional division probability by **LightGBM and
XGBoost seeded from a linear model's initial logit**, predictions averaged at equal weight; separate
models per pool. LightGBM probabilities build the initial graph, the averaged probabilities score
choices during optimisation. Note: their division calibration target requires an **exact match to the
parent and both daughters**, which differs from the official metric's local-lineage criterion.

## 9. Lineage graph optimisation (A3)

Their stated reason for going joint rather than sequential: *"If ordinary links are fixed first, a
correct daughter may be assigned to another parent, preventing a valid division from being added
later."* So node retention, ordinary links and daughter pairs are chosen **together**.

Constraints:

- each cell has at most one parent;
- each cell has at most one outgoing event — an ordinary link **or** a division;
- a division selects **both** daughters together;
- **a dividing parent must have an ordinary incoming link, and each daughter must have an ordinary
  outgoing link**;
- **a daughter cannot divide again at the immediately following time boundary**.

The last two reflect the dataset's temporal resolution, *"prevent implausible divisions from
supporting one another"*, and **strengthen the LP relaxation**.

**Objective = a surrogate of the competition metric itself**, computed from expected true positives,
false positives and node counts, then **linearised around the current solution** to yield per-edge
rewards and per-edge/per-node costs. Each video is optimised with those coefficients, expected counts
are aggregated **across videos**, coefficients are updated for **up to three rounds**, and an updated
solution is accepted **only if the surrogate improves**.

Solver: **HiGHS**, LP relaxation first; if fractional decisions remain, a **MILP with binary division
variables**; on time limit, keep a feasible solution. Optimisation runs on predicted probabilities and
the resulting graphs are scored separately with the official implementation.

## 10. Post-processing

- **A4 gap closing.** Connect track endpoints to the starts of later tracks: estimate collective
  motion from selected links, compensate, Hungarian assignment. Gaps of **one, two and three** missing
  frames processed **in that order**; for `g` missing frames the distance gate is **3·√(g+1) µm**.
  Nodes are **inserted** by linear interpolation between the original endpoint coordinates.
- **A5 short component removal.** Drop weakly connected components with **fewer than six nodes**,
  *after* gap closing, since a short fragment may become part of a longer track once missing
  detections are filled in.
- **A6 affine refinement of detected coordinates.** Graph fixed. Per consecutive frame pair, fit an
  affine model predicting displacement from position using ordinary links; a quadratic optimisation
  then balances consistency with predicted motion against staying near the original detection. Link
  weights are the **calibrated conditional correctness probabilities**. Division parents and daughters,
  gap endpoints and interpolated nodes are held fixed. Coordinates rounded to integer voxels; changes
  leaving the volume or colliding within a frame are rejected. Node and edge counts unchanged.
- **A7 affine refinement of interpolated coordinates.** Same affine fields, **fitted before** A6's
  refinement. Propagate from one endpoint through the motion fields and distribute the final endpoint
  discrepancy linearly across the gap so **both endpoints are preserved**. Only interpolated nodes
  move.

**A0 baseline detail:** consecutive-frame detections linked by physical distance, one-to-one Hungarian,
**7 µm gate**; collective translation (drift) estimated from the **median displacement of matched
points**, with drift correction and assignment repeated **twice**. No flow, no learned matcher, no
division prediction, no post-processing. A0–A2 retain all detections including isolated ones and
produce no branches, hence division Jaccard 0.

**Hungarian cost:** corrected distance `d` in A0/A1, and `d + 12(1 − p)` in A2 where `p` is the
matching probability. A0/A1 consider all pairs within 7 µm after drift correction; A2 applies the same
gate to matcher candidates, so the A1→A2 change **includes candidate filtering**. A2→A3 also moves the
gate from **7 µm to 12 µm** post-flow alongside calibration, division modelling, node selection and
joint optimisation.

Their own caution on reading the table: because ordinary-link calibration **also** consumes parent,
pre and post features, A3 introduces division modelling and graph optimisation **together**, and the
small A1/A2 rows *"do not capture their full role in the pipeline"* — flow distances and matching
scores feed the calibration that guides the LP.

## 12. Runtime engineering

TensorRT for the detectors and division models, mixed precision, parallel execution on **two GPUs**.
The matcher computes only the intermediate features it needs. Flow fields are reused for both matching
and division-image alignment. Division models **reuse encoder features of original frames across
overlapping temporal windows** — shared with the three-original-frame models and with the parent
model's original-frame branches, which additionally process the aligned images.
